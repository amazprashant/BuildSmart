At a Glance

This video, the opening lecture of CampusX's Agentic AI using LangGraph playlist, builds an intuition for what Agentic AI is by walking through a concrete hiring scenario and progressively upgrading the underlying solution. The journey moves through four stages: a plain LLM chatbot, a RAG-enhanced chatbot, a tool-augmented chatbot, and finally a true Agentic AI system. At every step we see a new limitation, fix it, and discover what the next stage must add.

By the end of the lecture you understand not just the textbook difference between Generative AI and Agentic AI, but why Agentic AI had to emerge — what each of its ingredients (tools, memory, planning, adaptability) actually solves.

1. Recap — What is Generative AI?

Generative AI refers to a class of AI models that can create new content — text, images, audio, code, or video — that resembles human-created data. It is a relatively new branch of AI, roughly three years old in mainstream form, and has already reshaped how people work, learn, and create.

1.1 Two reactions everyone has

  • Excitement — people see the obvious power of these tools.
  • A little fear — concern that the technology may displace jobs.

1.2 Popular Generative AI products

  • Chatbots: ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Grok — LLM-based assistants.
  • Image generation: DALL·E, Midjourney — diffusion-based models.
  • Code generation: Code Llama and similar fine-tuned LLMs.
  • Text-to-speech: ElevenLabs and other TTS systems.
  • Video generation: Sora and similar text-to-video models.

1.3 How GenAI differs from Traditional AI

Traditional AI (classical ML and deep learning) studies a labeled dataset and learns the input → output mapping. Generative AI does something fundamentally different: it learns the distribution of the data so it can sample new examples from that distribution.

In short: "Generative AI is about learning the distribution of data so that it can generate a new sample from it." The output feels human-made because the model has internalized what real data looks like.

1.4 Where GenAI is being applied today

  • Creative & business writing — blogs, business emails, polished drafts.
  • Software development — autocomplete, error explanation, refactoring.
  • Customer support — first-line chatbots that escalate to humans.
  • Education — personalized curricula, explanations, doubt-clearing inside videos.
  • Design — thumbnails, infographics, ad clips via tools like Sora and Runway.

2. The Practical Scenario — Hiring a Backend Engineer

To compare Generative AI and Agentic AI in a meaningful way, the video uses a single running example: you are an HR recruiter, and your company has asked you to hire a backend engineer with 2–4 years of experience. The full task breaks into six steps:

  • Draft the JD — write a detailed job description with skills, eligibility, salary, and expectations.
  • Post the JD — publish on platforms such as LinkedIn and Naukri.
  • Shortlist candidates — review résumés and pick the top 20–25.
  • Interview — schedule slots and conduct technical interviews.
  • Offer letter — draft and send the offer to the chosen candidate.
  • Onboarding — welcome email, contract, IT access, laptop, KT plan.

The same six steps will now be solved four different ways, and at each step we will note what improves and what is still broken.

3. Solution #1 — A Plain LLM Chatbot

Imagine the company has given the recruiter a generic LLM-based chatbot — something like ChatGPT. The recruiter walks through the hiring pipeline asking the chatbot for help at each stage:

  • Step 1: Ask the bot to draft a JD for a backend engineer with 2–4 yrs experience — it produces a generic JD.
  • Step 2: Ask where to post — the bot suggests LinkedIn, Naukri based on its training knowledge.
  • Step 3: Ask for help shortlisting — it gives generic screening tips (look for Python, cloud, startup experience).
  • Step 4: Ask the bot to draft an interview-invite email — it produces a polished email.
  • Step 5: Ask for sample interview questions — it returns a generic question bank.
  • Step 6: Ask for an offer-letter draft — it returns a generic offer letter.

So far so good — clearly better than the pre-GenAI era when the recruiter had to write everything from scratch. But four problems remain.

3.1 Problems with the plain chatbot

  • Reactive The bot only responds when prompted; it never initiates the next step.
  • No memory Three days later it has forgotten the earlier JD; the recruiter has to re-paste context.
  • Generic It has no knowledge of this specific company, its salary band, or its hiring playbook.
  • No actions It can write a JD, but it cannot post it on LinkedIn; it can draft an email, but cannot send it.

4. Solution #2 — A RAG-Enhanced Chatbot

To fix the "generic advice" problem, we connect the chatbot to the company's internal knowledge base. This pattern is known as Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): every time the user asks a question, the chatbot first retrieves relevant company documents and then generates an answer grounded in them.

4.1 What you feed into the knowledge base

  • Past JD templates — including high-performing ones and remote-vs-onsite, junior-vs-senior variants.
  • Hiring playbook — best platforms, best practices, internal salary bands, screening criteria.
  • Past interview question bank — what was actually asked at each level.
  • Onboarding documents — offer-letter templates, welcome emails, employee policies.

4.2 What changes in the workflow

Now when the recruiter asks for a JD, the chatbot already knows the company's stack is Python/Django, knows the salary band for 2–4 yrs, and knows the company tone — so the JD comes out tailored, not generic. Shortlisting tips are based on profiles the company has historically hired. Interview questions come from the company's own past interviews. Offer letters use the company's own format.

4.3 Problems still remaining

  • Solved Specific advice — RAG fixed this.
  • Open Reactive — still waits for prompts.
  • Open No memory — still forgets across sessions.
  • Open No actions — still cannot post jobs, send mail, or read résumés on its own.

5. Solution #3 — A Tool-Augmented Chatbot

The next limitation to attack is action-taking. We integrate the chatbot with real-world tools (APIs and software) so that — beyond replying with text — it can actually do things in the world.

5.1 Tools we connect

  • LinkedIn API — to post jobs, monitor applications, boost listings.
  • Résumé parser — to read and analyze PDFs of candidate résumés.
  • Calendar API — to check availability and book interview slots.
  • Email API — to send and receive interview invites, offers, follow-ups.
  • HRMS (HR Management System) — to trigger onboarding, contracts, IT access, laptop provisioning.

5.2 How the recruiter's workflow changes

  • JD drafting → still RAG-based, no tool needed.
  • Posting → bot calls LinkedIn + Naukri APIs directly. Recruiter does nothing.
  • Shortlisting → résumé parser downloads PDFs, scores them, returns ranked list.
  • Scheduling → bot reads recruiter's calendar, picks a slot, emails both parties.
  • Onboarding → bot triggers HRMS to generate contract, official email, laptop assignment, KT plan.

5.3 Problems still remaining

  • Solved Actions — tools fixed this.
  • Solved Specific advice — RAG still in play.
  • Open Reactive — bot still needs to be told what to do at each step.
  • Open No memory — still no persistent context.
  • Open No adaptability — when something goes wrong (low applications), the bot does not notice or act on its own.

6. Solution #4 — Agentic AI

The final upgrade is the conceptual leap. Take the tool-augmented chatbot and add three things: proactivity (it initiates), context-awareness (it remembers), and adaptability (it changes plans when reality changes). The result is no longer a chatbot at all — it is an AI agent.

Now the recruiter just provides a goal: "Hire a remote backend engineer with 2–4 years of experience." The agent does the rest.

6.1 What the agent does on its own

  • Understands the goal and breaks it into a plan: draft → post → monitor → screen → schedule → interview → offer → onboard.
  • Drafts a tailored JD using the RAG knowledge base; asks for human approval.
  • Auto-posts to LinkedIn and Naukri via APIs.
  • Continuously monitors application count. If too low, it diagnoses the problem and proposes adaptations (broaden JD to full-stack; boost the post).
  • Once enough applications arrive, the résumé parser ranks them; agent classifies as strong / partial / weak and surfaces the top candidates.
  • Reads the recruiter's calendar, schedules interviews, sends invites, mails reminders the day of.
  • Pulls a tailored question bank from past interviews.
  • Drafts the offer letter, sends it, tracks the reply.
  • Once accepted, triggers full onboarding via HRMS — welcome mail, IT access, laptop provisioning, intro meeting.

6.2 How problems are now solved

  • Solved Proactive — agent plans and acts on its own.
  • Solved Context-aware — has memory of every step in the workflow.
  • Solved Specific advice — RAG remains.
  • Solved Actions — full tool integration.
  • Solved Adaptable — recognizes problems and re-plans.

The recruiter's role compresses to monitoring and approving. The heavy lifting is fully automated. This is what an Agentic AI system looks like.

7. The Four-Stage Evolution at a Glance

Putting all four solutions side by side makes the progression obvious. Each stage adds one capability while keeping the previous ones.

8. Key Takeaways

8.1 The fundamental difference

  • Generative AI focuses on creating content — text, images, audio, code, or video. Its end goal is a generated artifact.
  • Agentic AI focuses on achieving goals — given a goal, it plans, executes step by step, and adapts.

8.2 Reactive vs. proactive

Generative AI is reactive: a human guides every step, the model responds. Agentic AI is proactive and autonomous: once you give it a goal, it drives the work itself, looping the human in only for approvals.

8.3 Generative AI is a building block of Agentic AI

Agentic AI is the broader system: it uses tools, planning, reasoning, and memory in addition to LLMs. The LLMs that power its reasoning come from the Generative AI world. So Generative AI sits inside Agentic AI as one of its capabilities.

"Generative AI is a capability; Agentic AI is a behavior built on top of many capabilities."

8.4 Why this matters

Watching the evolution from chatbot to RAG to tool-augmented to agent makes it clear why Agentic AI emerged as the next paradigm: each previous stage left visible gaps, and Agentic AI is the architecture that closes the last few — proactivity, persistent context, and adaptability — by combining everything we learned at the earlier stages.
